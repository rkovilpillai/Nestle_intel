Brand halo effect

One purchase opens the basket

When a Walmart shopper converts on a Nestlé product, what else do they buy in the same session? This cross-purchase data reveals the portfolio synergies your media plan isn't exploiting yet.

When a shopper buys Nescafé Clásico, they also buy...
Why this matters for your media plan
Cross-purchase data reveals portfolio synergies invisible in product-level reporting. A shopper who buys Nescafé is 34% likely to also buy Maggi in the same session — which means a Nescafé campaign is partially funding Maggi's conversion too.
Sequential targeting opportunity: Serve Maggi ads to shoppers who just purchased Nescafé. The basket affinity is already there.
Bundle creative: A "Nescafé + Maggi" combo creative on Onsite could capture both conversion events in a single impression. No other DSP has this signal.
Portfolio ROAS vs product ROAS: When you factor in halo conversions, Nescafé's effective ROAS is closer to 11× — not the reported 8.4×. This is the number to show in a QBR.

Three geographic insights
CDMX + Estado de México = 57% of all revenue — the metro area is the portfolio's anchor. Concentration risk: a single campaign pause here loses more than half the month's volume.
Nuevo León and Querétaro are efficiency outliers — both achieve ROAS above 8× at below-average CPA, yet receive less than 12% of the budget. These are under-invested markets with strong shopper intent.
Purina Dog Chow leads in Chihuahua and Sonora — northern states where pet ownership rates are highest. A geo-targeted Purina campaign in the north could yield 2× the conversion rate vs national targeting.
